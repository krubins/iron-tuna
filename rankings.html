Reading the board…

How to read the two lines under a name

Every row carries two sentences under the player’s name. Player is where he ranks at his own position on the board you have selected and at your scoring, and what he has actually done to get there: the points a game he has scored so far, the volume behind them, and whether that board’s projection backs that rate or marks it down, plus an injury or a usage swing where there is one. Before he has played there is nothing to report, and the line says what a rank like his is worth at his position instead. Opportunity is what is in front of him: on a one-week board the fixture, how hard the defense across from him has been on points allowed, and what the market implies his offense will score against its own season mean; on a longer board the games and byes he has left and how hard the defenses on that slate have been. A defense is graded on the other side of the fixture, on what the offense it faces is implied to score. Both sentences are built from the same numbers as the columns beside them, and a clause whose number the board does not carry is left out rather than guessed at.

Method

Where these numbers come from

Data & sources →

Iron Tuna prices players against the betting market first and the consensus projection feeds second. A sportsbook posts a number with its own money behind it and corrects it in public within minutes of news; a ranking carries no such penalty. When a priced market and an unpriced list disagree, the disagreement is the thing worth reading.

The week, the game states and the byes on this page come from the NFL schedule itself, not from the calendar: a week is current until its own last game has finished. Game lines and totals are the market’s, pulled on a schedule and cached, never fetched from your browser. Where a number is derived rather than quoted, the page says so.
