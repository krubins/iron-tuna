Reading the board…

How to read the two lines under a name

Every row carries two sentences under the player’s name. Player is where he ranks at his own position, then the one or two things about his season that are least like the rest of his position, each with his own numbers and his rank: his share of targets or touches, how much of his scoring came from touchdowns, his yards a catch or a carry, how often he catches his targets, his snaps, a swing in his workload last week, a projection well off the rate he has scored at, or betting odds that rank him well away from the consensus. Two players rarely get the same sentence, because two players are rarely unusual in the same way. One game is called one game rather than read, and an injury rides on the rank. Before he has played there is nothing to report, and the line says what a rank like his is worth at his position instead. Opportunity is what is in front of him: on a one-week board the fixture, how hard the defense across from him has been on points allowed, and what the market implies his offense will score against its own season mean; on a longer board whatever stands out most about the rest of his schedule (soft games now and hard ones later or the reverse, how weeks 15 to 17 grade, an offense projected near the top or bottom of the league, or a slate among the softest or hardest at his position), closed by the games and byes he has left. On either, any work a teammate’s absence leaves open. A defense is graded on the other side of the fixture, on what the offense it faces is implied to score. Both sentences are built from the same numbers as the columns beside them, and a clause whose number the board does not carry is left out rather than guessed at.

Where these numbers come from

Data & sources →

Iron Tuna prices players against the betting market first and the consensus projection feeds second. A sportsbook posts a number with its own money behind it and corrects it in public within minutes of news; a ranking carries no such penalty. When a priced market and an unpriced list disagree, the disagreement is the thing worth reading.

The week, the game states and the byes on this page come from the NFL schedule itself, not from the calendar: a week is current until its own last game has finished. Game lines and totals are the market’s, pulled on a schedule and cached, never fetched from your browser. Where a number is derived rather than quoted, the page says so.
