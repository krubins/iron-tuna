Snake Draft Insights: July 16, 2026

Evan Brooks·Fantasy Rankings Analyst·Iron Tuna·July 16, 2026·5 calls, snake edition

Featuring Josh Allen, Arizona, Jahmyr Gibbs, Puka Nacua, and Tyler Warren.

Also published in an auction edition.

01
UnderpricedQBHigh confidence

Josh Allen remains the safest overall QB1, but not the best pure value

Safety has a price, and remarkably it's still a fair one. Allen finished 2025 as the QB1 at 22.0 FPPG, and consensus projects him for 372.2 standard points — first at the position again. Back-to-back top billing from both results and projections is what a floor looks like: a scoring base this stable means even his bad weeks clear what most quarterbacks call good ones.

The projected zero-to-four percent edge versus price says the market has him roughly right, so the argument for drafting him isn't profit, it's variance reduction — you're buying the position's most reliable weekly output and spending your risk budget elsewhere. Confidence in that framing is high.

The call strengthens in builds where a steady positional edge compounds week after week, and weakens only if your roster construction depends on finding surplus at quarterback rather than certainty.

The play: Move the player or group up roughly one round, or to the front of the current tier. Draft him if you want floor dominance, not because he is discounted.

Projected effect: +0% to +4% versus price

02
OverpricedMarketHigh confidence

Arizona is the clearest macro fade because the offense got worse and the schedule got harder

Quarterback downgrade plus schedule pain is the ugliest combination in team-level analysis, and Arizona has both. Releasing Kyler Murray and handing the offense to Jacoby Brissett lowers the ceiling on every skill player at once — fewer explosive plays, more conservative game plans, more punts.

The Jeremiyah Love pick suggests a run-leaning identity, and the slate compounds everything: the hardest projected schedule in schedule modeling plus an official top-five toughest slate means the environment fights this offense weekly. Tough schedules suppress scoring directly, through better defenses, and indirectly, through game scripts that shorten games. A five-to-ten percent drag on the whole scoring environment justifies discounting nearly every Cardinal.

Trey McBride is the exception, because target dominance can beat a bad team context. Confidence is high — only an unexpected quarterback change or the schedule playing softer than modeled rescues the rest.

The play: Do not reach; consider only if the player or group falls at least a round below market cost. Draft Trey McBride because he can beat team weakness, but haircut the rest.

Projected effect: -5% to -10% to broad Cardinals scoring environment

03
OverpricedRBHigh confidence

Jahmyr Gibbs is appropriately expensive

Paying full freight for a running back is usually how auctions are lost, but Gibbs is the case where the sticker is honest. He finished RB4 in 2025 PPR and projects for 301.5 standard points — the top RB projection on the board — so the market's aggressive price is simply tracking the evidence.

A projected band of minus-one to plus-three percent versus cost means you aren't finding a bargain; you're buying stability at fantasy's most volatile position, which has real portfolio value even without surplus. Practically, don't chase him past your number, and don't expect the room to let him slide either. Confidence that he's priced correctly is high.

Format matters here — full PPR and deeper leagues reward his reception-driven profile most, and the shape of your auction budget should decide whether a fairly priced anchor fits the build you're constructing.

The play: Do not reach; consider only if the player or group falls at least a round below market cost. Pay up if you want stability, but do not expect a big discount.

Projected effect: -1% to +3% versus price

04
Format-dependentWRHigh confidence

Puka Nacua is expensive and still deserves it

Leading all wide receivers with 375 PPR points and projecting first again in 2026 receiving fantasy points is about as clean a resume as pricing models ever see, and it explains why Nacua costs what he costs.

The zero-to-five percent projected edge says the market has him essentially right, with the tilt slightly in your favor — a first-place projection on top of a first-place finish means the modeling doesn't treat last season as a fluke. The practical takeaway is discipline in both directions: don't overpay past your sheet, and don't talk yourself into a clever fade of production this established. Confidence in the fair-value read is high.

Format sharpens it — full PPR maximizes his catch-driven scoring, and in larger leagues the scarcity of true set-and-forget WR1s makes paying a fair price far more palatable than hunting a cheaper approximation.

The play: Treat as tier-dependent and avoid forcing the pick. No need to galaxy-brain fade.

Projected effect: 0% to +5% versus market

05
UnderpricedTEHigh confidence

Tyler Warren is one of the best breakout TE values

A TE4 finish in 2025 PPR isn't a projection — it's a receipt, and the market is still pricing Warren like the breakout hasn't happened yet. That's the gap worth attacking. Tight end scoring is brutally top-heavy, so a player who has already delivered a top-four season while carrying the profile of the position's best breakout candidate gives you elite-week equity without an elite-tier cost.

The projected 10-to-14 percent edge versus price means he can return early-round production from a materially later investment, which frees your top picks for running back and receiver capital. Confidence is high because the case rests on a demonstrated finish plus profile rather than pure projection. What would weaken it: a genuine role change in his offense.

What strengthens it: any drift downward in his cost while McBride and Bowers get pushed up boards.

The play: Move the player or group up roughly one round, or to the front of the current tier. Priority target if you miss McBride/Bowers.

Projected effect: +10% to +14% versus price
