How these trades are judged

A roster is worth what it starts. Every player is projected at your scoring from the same board the rankings use, and each team’s lineup is filled the way your league fills it: the named slots first, then flex and superflex from whoever is left. The bench counts a little, as insurance, and a backup quarterback in a one-QB league counts for almost nothing. Kickers and defenses are left out.

Every team in the deal is scored. For a two-, three- or four-team trade, each team’s lineup is filled before and after, counting what it sends and what it receives. A team that gains three quarters of a point a week or more is called a gain, one that loses as much a loss, and anything between is about even. When the deal is not a yes for everyone, the finder shows the closest trades among the same teams that are.

Finding a trade. Leave the trade boxes empty and the engine tries every one-, two- and three-player swap between your team and each of the others, keeping only swaps where both lineups improve by at least three quarters of a point a week. The balance slider orders what survives: at Even by the smaller of the two gains, at In my favor by yours alone. It cannot surface a trade the other manager should refuse.

Horizons are per team. A gain is measured on the weeks that team is playing for — this week, the next three, the rest of the season, or the fantasy playoffs — and stated per week so a three-week horizon and a twelve-week one are on the same scale.

Your notes. Records, standings and the other factors you type are read by a language model after the numbers are in. It explains whether they should change the call; it never changes a projection, and the scores above stand on their own.

What it does not know. It cannot see your league’s roster limits, its trade deadline, who is on injured reserve, or which manager is unreachable. A team that receives more players than it sends needs roster spots; the card says so. Screenshots are read by a model into names only, and every name is then matched to the board here in your browser, so nothing it read can become a projection on its own.
