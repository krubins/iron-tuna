    Vegas vs. Consensus

    See it on your cheat sheet →

    Iron Tuna prices every player against the betting market. Almost nobody else does. These are the players where that changes the number — recomputed from the live lines, with a new case every six hours.

    Why the odds get a vote

    A sportsbook posts a number with its own money behind it. Get it wrong and sharp bettors take the other side until it moves, so every line is priced off repeatable trends, statistical modeling, and injury and usage news the moment it lands — then corrected in public, continuously.

    A ranking carries no such penalty. Anyone with a TikTok account and a hunch can publish a top 200, never revisit it, and pay nothing when it misses. That is not an argument that the book is always right. It is an argument that when a priced market and an unpriced list disagree, the disagreement is worth a look.

    Iron Tuna’s prices already carry the odds — every value on your cheat sheet is blended toward the market. Almost no ranking or ADP list does that. So each case here is the gap between what the consensus is charging you and what the lines say the player is worth.

    1. Read the implied points, not the spread. A spread and a total together give each offense its expected points per game — the scoring environment every skill player on that roster inherits.
    2. Compare like with like. The useful question is never “is this team good?” but whether the market ranks that offense higher or lower than the consensus projections already do.
    3. Buy the gap, not the name. The edge is the distance between a player’s consensus price and the market-implied one — a cheap player the odds like is worth more than a stud everyone already agrees on.
    4. Respect the clamp. One weird line is noise. Treat a gap as a tiebreaker between players you already rate closely, not a reason to blow up your board.

      How Iron Tuna reads the market

      Iron Tuna’s proprietary formulas translate spreads, over/unders, player props and related betting information into projections. The model then blends that market read with other projection inputs and evaluates line movement before deciding how much weight the movement deserves.

      1. Observe

      The posted market

      Totals, spreads and player props are observations. They are kept separate from anything Iron Tuna derives from them.

      2. Translate

      Odds into projections

      Iron Tuna converts market prices into expected football outcomes, then scores those outcomes into fantasy terms where applicable.

      3. Filter

      Signal versus noise

      Movement gets context. Broad, corroborated movement can earn more weight; popularity-driven or isolated movement can be discounted rather than blindly followed.

      Methodology note: a sportsbook line is not an Iron Tuna projection, and an Iron Tuna projection is not a sportsbook quote. The source and basis should remain visible anywhere the two appear together.
