In-Season · Depth Charts

Every team’s depth chart, refreshed every day

Who starts, who is behind him, and who moves up when the man in front of him goes down. The order below is the league’s own published depth chart, pulled fresh every day, with each player’s current injury designation carried on his name. It is the same feed the site’s Value Coach answers depth questions from, so the page and the coach can never tell you two different things.

Reading the depth charts…

How to read it

Each club is listed in its published order at the four positions a fantasy roster starts: quarterback, running back, receiver and tight end. The three receiver slots teams publish separately — split end, flanker and slot — are merged into one order here, because the question a manager is actually asking is who the second and third receivers are, not who lines up on which side of the formation.

A letter after a name is the current designation on the league’s injury report: Q questionable, D doubtful, O out, IR injured reserve, PUP physically unable to perform, SUS suspended. Hover a letter for the designation in full and, where the feed names one, the body part. A name with no letter carries no designation as of the last refresh, which is not the same as fully healthy — it is what the report says.

What a depth chart will not tell you

It is the order a club publishes, not a snap count. A committee backfield can list a clear RB1 and split carries down the middle; a slot receiver behind two starters can out-target both. Read the order for what it settles — who is in front of whom, and who inherits the touches if that changes — and read the usage numbers for how the work was actually divided.

The order also moves faster than any feed. A Wednesday practice report and a Friday designation change it, and a Sunday inactive list changes it again ninety minutes before kickoff. This page is as current as the last refresh stamped above it and no more current than that.
