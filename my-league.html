Rankings, the Pickup Advisor, Best Lineup, the Trade Finder and the stories all read your league instead of a default one. Free. Sign in with an email link; no purchase is required.

What Iron Tuna keeps, and what it never asks for

What you enter: the league’s name and size, its scoring and starting lineup, the FAAB budget, team names and the players on each roster. That is all, and it is all typed, pasted or read off a screenshot by you — Iron Tuna does not connect to Sleeper, Yahoo, ESPN or CBS, holds no password or access token for any of them, and makes no request to any fantasy platform on your behalf. The roster-grid screenshot in section 02 is sent to Iron Tuna’s reader to be transcribed and is not kept. Your league is used only to personalize your own guidance and is never shown to other readers. Deleting a league removes its settings, teams, rosters and history. Full details in the privacy policy.

02

Your in-season settings

Platform, scoring, lineup, teams and FAAB budget. Every board in the In-Season section restates its numbers at these, whether or not a league is connected. If you already set your league up for the cheat sheet, this page starts from that rather than asking you the same questions twice, and only puts the boxes in front of you that the cheat sheet cannot answer. Otherwise there are three: scoring, your league’s rosters, and the FAAB budget. Every box takes the same two things — type or paste the text, or paste a screenshot straight off the clipboard — and shows you what it read as values to check before anything moves. The FAAB budget is the one number most tools guess at: a $23 bid out of $100 and a $23 bid out of $1,000 are not the same decision.

What the draft app knows

Your cheat sheet carries the same full scoring — every yardage divisor and touchdown value — and it is what the draft board prices against. Where one is set up in this browser, the form on the left opens already filled in from it rather than asking you for it twice.

Iron Tuna prices players against the betting market first and the consensus projection feeds second. A sportsbook posts a number with its own money behind it and corrects it in public within minutes of news; a ranking carries no such penalty. When a priced market and an unpriced list disagree, the disagreement is the thing worth reading.

The week, the game states and the byes on this page come from the NFL schedule itself, not from the calendar: a week is current until its own last game has finished. Game lines and totals are the market’s, pulled on a schedule and cached, never fetched from your browser. Where a number is derived rather than quoted, the page says so.
